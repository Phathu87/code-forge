# Security incident response

Status: operational runbook drafted 2026-09-16; not yet exercised. No incident is asserted by this audit. Launch is blocked until named primary/backup incident commander, security responder, operations responder, privacy/legal reviewer and communications owner accept responsibility, publish a monitored security contact and verify paging. Do not put personal contacts or evidence in this public repository.

Trigger this plan for suspected impact to user data, credentials, learner source, certificates, integrity evidence or execution infrastructure. Treat suspected cross-tenant exposure, execution escape, signing/provider key exposure or substantial source loss as critical until scoped. Any operator may escalate; only the designated commander closes an incident.

1. **Containment:** Open a restricted incident record with ID, UTC discovery time, reporter and commander. Restrict the affected endpoint, execution pool, publishing or issuance capability using operator controls; isolate suspect workers and suspend affected jobs. Stop ongoing harm first. Record every containment action and its effect. Do not run hostile probes on production.
2. **Evidence preservation:** Before restart, rebuild, rollback or cleanup where safe, preserve immutable copies of relevant logs, job IDs, deployment/configuration versions, database/storage snapshots and volatile worker evidence. Disable destructive log rotation for the affected evidence under a legal hold. Record UTC collection time, collector, source, SHA-256, storage location, access history and custody transfers. Store originals read-only in a restricted evidence store; investigate copies. Do not commit source, tokens, personal data or full evidence to Git. If immediate containment loses volatile evidence, record why and what was lost. Never wipe evidence just to restore service faster.
3. **Credential rotation:** Inventory potentially exposed sessions, database/provider/API keys and signing material. Revoke compromised credentials/sessions promptly; preserve identifiers and audit metadata, not plaintext secrets in tickets. Replace via the secret manager, update dependent services, verify old credentials are rejected and new credentials work. Review signing-key compromise with the relevant platform owner. Record scope and completion; never merely delete a leaked file.
4. **Scope determination:** Establish earliest/latest known activity, affected releases, tenants/resources and actors from evidence. Inspect lateral access, exports, public portfolios, certificate issuance/revocation and integrity-record tampering. Separate confirmed facts, hypotheses, unknowns and confidence. Do not infer zero impact from absent logs.
5. **Service recovery:** Recover into clean infrastructure using reviewed artifacts and rotated credentials. Restore to an isolated environment first; verify record counts, source hashes, tenant authorization and certificate/evidence integrity. Preserve unsynced work; do not overwrite newer learner data blindly. Commander and operations approve staged restoration, smoke tests and heightened monitoring. Roll back traffic if symptoms recur; database recovery needs its own plan.
6. **User-impact assessment:** Document affected data categories, users, duration, availability/integrity/confidentiality impact and actions users can take. Privacy/legal owner determines notification duties and deadlines for applicable jurisdictions and contracts; track decisions and evidence. This runbook does not invent a universal deadline.
7. **Required communications:** Communications owner and commander approve status-page/support messages and any required private notifications. State confirmed affected services, investigation status, mitigation and next update time. Say scope is under investigation when unknown. Do not claim no data was affected without support. Keep exploit details, secrets and personal data private. Archive sent versions and recipients securely. No messages are sent automatically by this repository.
8. **Remediation:** Assign owners and due dates for root cause, detection gaps, data repairs, authorization fixes and regression tests. Preserve immutable original assessment evidence; append corrections and certificate revocations with audit history. Re-run affected release gates against the new artifact.
9. **Post-incident review:** Commander schedules a blameless review, records timeline, root causes, impact, response gaps and action items; validates fixes and monitoring. Security/privacy authorize evidence-retention and legal-hold disposition before any eventual deletion. Closure requires sign-off and tracked remaining risks.

## Incident record template

ID / severity / commander / opened UTC / next update UTC; affected environment and build; confirmed facts / hypotheses / unknowns; containment timeline; evidence inventory and custody; credential identifiers and rotation results; scope and impact; recovery/validation evidence; notification decisions and approved communications; remediation owners/dates; review and closure sign-offs. Keep this filled record in restricted incident storage, not the public report.

## Exercise before launch

In isolated staging, rehearse a leaked test credential plus compromised execution worker. Demonstrate paging, containment, preserved evidence hashes, old-key rejection, clean recovery, impact assessment and approved communication drafting. Record elapsed detection/containment/recovery times and follow-up defects. A written plan alone does not pass operational readiness.
